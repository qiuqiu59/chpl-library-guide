# Chapel Hill Public Library Budget Analysis (FY 2025–26)

This repository contains an analysis of the Chapel Hill Public Library's financial structure, revenue sources, and operational trends based on the official Town of Chapel Hill **FY 2025–26 Adopted Budget** (`202526-adopted-budget.pdf`).

---

## 📌 Executive Summary

Although the library's total adopted budget shows a nominal increase reaching **$4.98M** in FY 2025–26, the library is experiencing an **operational and purchasing power squeeze**:
- **Personnel & Benefits Inflation**: 78.3% ($3.90M) of the budget is consumed by fixed personnel costs (health insurance +6%, retirement +0.75%, and salary adjustments).
- **Stagnant Intergovernmental Aid**: Support from Orange County ($621,323) and State Aid ($39,000) remains frozen at flat levels.
- **Grant Revenue Decline**: External project grant funding in the Library Gift Fund dropped from **$80,197 (FY24)** to **$0 (FY26)**.
- **Service Contraction**: Technology lending is down ~65%, physical visits per capita have steadily declined, and materials collection spending remains below the state average benchmark of 10%.

## 🛠️ Actionable Solutions & Recommendations

1. **Modernize County Funding Agreement**:
   - Transition from a static flat grant ($621,323) to a formula-based agreement indexed to cardholder demographics and regional population growth.
2. **Reinvigorate External Grant Pursuits**:
   - Re-establish regular grant application pipelines (IMLS, LSTA, state/private foundations) to recover the missing $60k–$80k+ non-tax revenue stream.
3. **Endowment & Planned Giving Strategy**:
   - Partner with the *Friends of Chapel Hill Public Library* to cultivate long-term endowment funds, providing consistent annual operating returns.
4. **Facility & Resource Optimization**:
   - Expand fee-based meeting space rentals for commercial/private functions during off-hours while maintaining community access.
5. **Consortium Digital Licensing**:
   - Leverage shared regional purchasing consortiums for e-books and databases to lower unit subscription costs and free up funding for physical collections.
